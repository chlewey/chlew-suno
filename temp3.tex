\songtitle{Hear!}
% -> stories.tex (African Empire -- Monukae); original.tex (Other Narratives)
\tag*{2026}\tag{medieval-ballad}\tag{original-lyrics}\tag{english-lyrics}\tag{african-empire}\tag{monukae}\tag{mombuto}
\begin{notes}
Written by Carlos Thompson -- a first-person ballad by Mombuto, from the \emph{African Empire} alternate history (the \emph{Monukae} series): the fleet lord who fought the Romans, was knighted by the emperor he served, and came home to Monukae to see his lineage merge into the Malian imperial line. Retrieved from Carlos Th's Suno page (V6, 3 October 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Medieval ballad, close-miked plucked strings with intimate finger and pick detail, measured bass-march pulse, sustained drones beneath kora, lauds and harps, male bass vocals in a resonant, restrained delivery using the user's own recorded voice.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
[Verse 1]
Hear!

I once fought the Romans,
now they knighted me for my service.
They once killed my father—
no blame, that's war on the surface.
Burn in Roman fire
during the last siege in the Aegean Sea,
the same sea my defence
was a duty I fulfilled with glee.

[Verse 2]
Hear!

We defeated the Romans,
but my emperor, my brother-in-arms,
feared my father, his mentor,
and feared me, my fleet and my farms.
I dismantled my rights
to my land and my fleet for his sake;
I put myself to his duty,
and the Roman issue was the highest stake.

[Verse 3]
Hear!

Those Romans who crossed us
were defeated and properly dealt with;
new Romans under Mali
were our new allies and they agreed swith.
I never shifted loyalties—
rather, loyalties shifted around me—
and my beloved Gladiel
rebuilt it while I played diplomacy.

[Verse 4]
Hear!

I took Byzantine Christ
just as I took the Christ of the Franks,
and when Arabs attacked Rome
I gave the Romans my new fleet and my ranks.
They took me as their own;
the emperor knighted Gladiel and me both,
but glee wouldn't endure—
disease found her soon, betraying my troth.

[Verse 5]
Hear!

I left my sons in Byzantium
and came back to Monukae, retired for good.
My first wife also passed,
but my daughter kept the land; she understood.
A lineage of mine goes on,
being merged in the Malian imperial line;
my other lineage keeps the fleet
from the Black Sea to the Caribbean shine.
\end{lyrics}

\songtitle{My Quiet Genius}
% -> entourage-eco.tex (Characters); original.tex (Thomas' Entourage)
\tag*{2026}\tag{synth-pop}\tag{original-lyrics}\tag{thomas-entourage}\tag{gabriele-wiater}\label{sec:my-quiet-genius}
\begin{notes}
From the \emph{Thomas’ Entourage} narrative universe by Carlos Thompson. Original lyrics, in the voice of Gabriele Wiater -- a Simpsons episode with Hank Scorpio sets off a portrait of Thomas: a quiet genius and clueless polymath who holds whole systems in his mind yet is unaware of his power over the people around him. Retrieved from Gabriel's Suno page (V6-MINI, 3 October 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Synth pop with tape-saturated synth layers, metallic industrial percussion and buzzing machine textures; steady midtempo pulse, female lead with fluid R&B phrasing, intimate verses and layered chorus vocals.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
[Verse 1]
I was watching this episode of The Simpsons,
the one in which Hank Scorpio works with Homer.
Something in Scorpio’s moods just felt
the way my Thomas sometimes behaves.
My Scorpio is not a supervillain, although
somebody might well disagree.
He’s neither the most interesting man in the world,
but certainly very interesting to me.

[Chorus]
My quiet genius, my clueless polymath,
holding whole systems in his mind
from the overall picture to the finest detail,
but also oblivious of his surrounding kind.

[Verse 2]
He treats people around him with proper respect:
not a bad word, not a reprimand, no undeserved foul.
If you cross him, you’ve lost him—he’ll let you go;
you’ll feel your fault like an ache in your gut.
He doesn’t want to control you;
he wouldn’t know what to do with control.
He’s just not aware of his power over people—
not something he cares about, but part of his toll.

[Chorus]
My quiet genius, my clueless polymath,
holding whole systems in his mind
from the overall picture to the finest detail,
but also oblivious of his surrounding kind.

[Bridge]
If he puts you in a label,
it’s for him to understand.
If he puts you in a contract,
it’s for him to know where he stands.
He’s not putting you in constraints;
he’s not demanding you to be
nothing but truly you—
that’s your admission fee.

[Final Chorus]
My quiet genius, my clueless polymath,
holding whole systems in his mind.
He gets the big picture;
he gets the finest detail;
he even gets what he’s oblivious about.
Understanding people and his own power—
he can know it; he’s just not aware.
My quiet genius,
my clueless polymath.
\end{lyrics}

\songtitle{The Questions Kept On Shaping}

\tag*{2026}\tag{chamber-punk}\tag{original-source}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by SUNO based on a Gemini Notebook summary of Carlos Thompson's mathematics-olympiad career, itself based on his memoir \emph{A Life in World Events} -- from a first-grade head start in Sweden to the national contest, four international olympiads, and the 2013 olympiad hosted in Colombia. Retrieved from Carlos Th's Suno page (V6, 3 October 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Urgent chamber punk anthem, brisk straight-ahead punk-rock pulse with driving kick and snare, distorted power-chord guitar, bowed cello doubling the bass movement, clipped violin and viola counterlines, and sharp ensemble stabs. Start with a tense string ostinato and close, determined lead vocal; bring in the full band for compact narrative verses, then open into a shouted, melodic gang-backed chorus. Use a brief stripped-down strings-and-voice bridge before the final chorus. Raw live-room energy, clear vocal up front, punchy drums, gritty guitars, tightly arranged chamber strings.
\end{style}

While the room learned numbers, I was turning pages
Back in Colombia, school days shifted course
Then eighth grade came, and the contest doors

[Chorus]
