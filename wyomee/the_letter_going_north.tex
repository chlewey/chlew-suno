\songtitle{The Letter Going North}

\setin{1799}

\begin{notes}
Lyrics by ChatGPT based on a scene of \emph{British Louisiana} (2003), an alternate history by Carlos Thompson (Part 6, \emph{Troubles in Florida}) -- the governor's complaint carried from Saint Augustine to Louisville in August 1799, while the news of the massacre travels faster. Retrieved from Wyomee Dank's Suno page (V6-MINI, 24 November 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Circa-1800 Southern backcountry folk ballad; one unaccompanied low tenor, light Southern inflection and plain diction, with a powerful full-voice belt on held line endings; dry, sparse recording with silence beneath the final refrain; steady riding pace and room to linger at line ends; a narrow recurring melody repeats unchanged, with a short, dry fiddle turn between stanzas.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1 - Solo}
I saw the smoke at Las Aguas\\
Before the sun sank low.\\
A woman dragged her bedding out\\
And asked which way to go.

\songpart{Verse 2 - Solo}
At Saint Augustine before dawn\\
I took the letter sealed.\\
I kept it buttoned in my coat\\
Across the first wet field.

\annotation{Refrain - Solo}\\
I bore the letter north by hand,\\
I kept the wax seal whole.\\
The word flew on ahead of me\\
And paid no man his toll.

\annotation{Short fiddle interlude}

\songpart{Verse 3 - Solo}
By dusk I saw another town\\
Burn down beside the road.\\
A family passed me in a cart\\
With ash upon the load.

\songpart{Verse 4 - Solo}
The ferryman looked back at me.\\
“A third town lies ahead.\\
Two hundred took that road last night.\\
Ride wide of them,” he said.

\annotation{Refrain - Solo}\\
I bore the letter north by hand,\\
I kept the wax seal whole.\\
The word flew on ahead of me\\
And paid no man his toll.

\annotation{Short fiddle interlude}

\songpart{Verse 5 - Solo}
At Louisville I found the clerk\\
And laid it by his light.\\
I watched him break the seal and read\\
The news of the first night.

\annotation{Final Refrain - Solo, unaccompanied}\\
I bore the letter north by hand,\\
I kept the wax seal whole.\\
The word flew on ahead of me\\
And paid no man his toll.
