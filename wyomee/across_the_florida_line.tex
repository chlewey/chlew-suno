\begin{style}
Southern soul ballad with an Afro-Caribbean groove; low, dark-register lead vocal, close and expressive, with final-refrain call-and-response; dry, intimate vocal production, ending unaccompanied; warm electric piano leads over deep syncopated bass, hand-played congas, shaker, and quiet wood clave, percussion entering beneath the first refrain; patient 78 BPM sway, restrained under the fire news.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1 - Lead vocal and electric piano}
I woke on a borrowed blanket\\
With daylight on my feet.\\
A woman called from the doorway,\\
“Come get yourself something to eat.”

Her name was Ruth. She drew me water.\\
She set the cup by me.\\
She waited while my hands quit shaking,\\
Then asked what she should call me.

\annotation{Refrain - Bass and hand percussion enter}\\
Across the Florida line,\\
I said my name out loud.\\
She asked me what to call me.\\
She never asked whose I was.

\songpart{Verse 2 - Full groove, soft}
I took the pail down to the well.\\
Two soldiers passed in blue.\\
One tipped his hat and kept on walking.\\
I stood and watched him go.

Then I took the long way back to Ruth’s,\\
Past every open door.\\
I used to walk that slow on Sundays,\\
If there was no one keeping score.

\annotation{Refrain}\\
Across the Florida line,\\
I said my name out loud.\\
She asked me what to call me.\\
She never asked whose I was.

\songpart{Verse 3 - Low percussion, no backing vocals}
At dusk a man came up the road\\
With soot along his sleeve.\\
He said they’d burned Las Aguas down.\\
I asked who’d got away.

Ruth brought another cup of water.\\
He held it in both hands.\\
I’d spent the morning learning how\\
To walk slow on this land.

\songpart{Bridge - Voice and hand percussion}
If my sister comes from Georgia\\
With the road still in her feet,\\
I’ll set a cup beside her\\
The way Ruth did for me.
