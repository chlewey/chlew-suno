\songtitle{No Griot for Thee}

\tag*{2026}\tag{rebetiko}\tag{original-lyrics}\tag{african-empire}\tag{monukae}\tag{abulon}\tag{mombuto}\tag{gladiel}
\begin{notes}
Written by Carlos Thompson -- Mombuto's own perspective within the \emph{Monukae} saga, living in Abulon's shadow: raised to read power and war rather than to be sung about like his father, torn between his land and his navy, his Peruvian wife and his Irish wife (Gladiel), and a legacy he built without ever earning his own griot's song. Retrieved from Wyomee Dank's Suno page (V6-MINI, 24 September 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Rebetiko with tape saturation and plate reverb; bouzouki tremolo, baglama strums, upright bass, qanun runs, frame drum patterns, orchestral strings, brass fanfare, and choral choir; male baritone lead with rubato phrasing and half-sung outro; sweeping 6/8 marcha in minor-key harmonies, symphonic drama, imperial melancholy
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Intro}
How do you live under expectations\\
when your father was a force of nature?

\songpart{Verse 1 - male baritone}
They told me I was born in the sea\\
my mother a princess from the far land\\
my father the heir of a general\\
who married into the richest dukedom in Mali

My father had to fight back for his domain\\
when usurpers took control of Monukae\\
a fight I can't remember, I was too young\\
but that's the foundation under my vain.

\songpart{Chorus - choir}
Mombuto, Mombuto, son of Abulon\\
Heir of Monukae who renounced all\\
Mombuto, Mombuto, grandson of the Inca,\\
general of Mali, chief diplomat,\\
knight of the Romans,\\
but no griot for thee

\songpart{Verse 2 - male baritone}
I grew up learning to ride and to read the wave,\\
but mostly learning how to read people,\\
how to understand power and leverage,\\
and how to understand war—and I was quite brave.

But I was not my grandfather, nor my father\\
where I excelled was not what the griot sings\\
my father's success was my lever, also my curse\\
and I managed at the end to build my own ledger.

\songpart{Chorus - choir}
Mombuto, Mombuto, son of Abulon\\
Heir of Monukae who renounced all\\
Mombuto, Mombuto, grandson of the Inca,\\
general of Mali, chief diplomat,\\
knight of the Romans,\\
but no griot for thee.

\songpart{Breakdown - male baritone}
My father was a force of nature,\\
slayer of his enemies, the shadow king\\
who won wars even in his death.\\
My brother-in-arms, my playmate,\\
taught by my father just at my side.\\
As the new emperor he feared\\
I was my father—I was not.\\
But he forced me to choose: my land or my navy.\\
I surrendered my land to my Peruvian wife,\\
my business to my Irish wife,\\
and the merchant navy to the emperor.

\songpart{Chorus - choir}
Mombuto, Mombuto, son of Abulon\\
Heir of Monukae who renounced all\\
Mombuto, Mombuto, grandson of the Inca,\\
general of Mali, chief diplomat,\\
knight of the Romans,\\
grandfather of future emperors,\\
and of a new dynasty of the sea,\\
but no griot for thee.

\songpart{Outro - male baritone, half-sung}
I served my Malian emperor\\
I then served the Roman one\\
With my Irish wife I rebuilt the merchant navy\\
my daughter merged Monukae with the royal line\\
my sons took over from their mother,\\
becoming kings of the sea\\
and I lived a life of adventures\\
but always under the shadow of Abulon
\end{lyrics}
