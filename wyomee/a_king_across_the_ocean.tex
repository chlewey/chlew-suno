\songtitle{A King Across the Ocean}

\setin{1780}
\tag*{2003}\tag{folk-ballad}\tag{multilingual}\tag{original-source}\tag{british-louisiana}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by ChatGPT based on a scene of \emph{British Louisiana} (2003), an alternate history by Carlos Thompson (Part 2, \emph{Loyalist Louisiana}) -- a French-speaking Louisianan explains to an American republican why Louisiana kept the Crown. Retrieved from Wyomee Dank's Suno page (V6-MINI, 24 November 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Folk ballad, two alternating warm mid-range singers with crooning legato phrasing, close-miked dry vocals and no overlap, unhurried two-beat sway, open-string-drone fiddle and soft heel tap with spacious arrangement; fiddle falls silent in the bridge, Canadian ends alone and unaccompanied.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1 - Canadian, English}
You ask me why we kept the Crown\\
When you sent yours away.\\
The men who rule from overseas\\
Leave much to us from day to day.

Our priest still keeps the parish books.\\
We settle what we can.\\
If your assembly makes the rules,\\
Who gets a seat? Who keeps the land?

\annotation{Refrain - Canadian}\\
A king across the ocean,\\
A republic at my door.\\
Quand tu me parles de liberté,\\
J’ai la gorge qui se serre.\\
J’entends encore ma mère chanter,\\
Pis j’ai peur de devoir me taire.

\annotation{Short fiddle interlude}

\songpart{Verse 2 - American, English}
We had no vote across the sea,\\
Though every tax came due.\\
I wanted some say in what I paid.\\
I thought you’d want that too.

\songpart{Verse 3 - Canadian, English}
I do. But when you speak for us,\\
Who told you that you could?\\
You’ve known my mind for half an hour.\\
You say you’ve understood.

\songpart{Bridge - Canadian, French, fiddle falls silent}
Dans la chambre, mon petit chante\\
L’air que ma mère m’a laissé.\\
J’ai mis la main sur le loquet.\\
J’ai pas eu le cœur d’entrer.

\songpart{Verse 4 - Canadian, English, quietly}
You said you ought to head back south.\\
I put more wood upon the fire.\\
I still had questions you could ask.\\
Your boots could stand to dry.

\songpart{Outro - Canadian, French, unaccompanied}
Reste encore un peu, mon ami.\\
Écoute comme il chante.\\
C’est l’air que ma mère m’a laissé.\\
Il le sait mieux que moi.
