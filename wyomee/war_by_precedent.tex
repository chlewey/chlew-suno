\songtitle{War by Precedent}

\setin{1800}
\tag*{2003}\tag{folk-ballad}\tag{original-source}\tag{british-louisiana}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by ChatGPT based on a scene of \emph{British Louisiana} (2003), an alternate history by Carlos Thompson (Part 7, \emph{Georgia at War!}) -- a clerk of Congress in Philadelphia weighing the two accounts of the border killings and the precedent of backing Georgia's war with Spain. Retrieved from Wyomee Dank's Suno page (V6-MINI, 24 November 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Late-1790s political broadside ballad, spare fiddle and a short featured fife turn between sections; one dry, clear baritone, powerful full-voice belt on the melody’s peaks with brief whispered breaths between lines, alternating eight- and six-syllable lines with matter-of-fact delivery; close, unadorned mix, both reports sung to the same narrow repeating tune, refrain rising slightly before its unaccompanied last verse and refrain; steady duple pulse.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1 - Solo}
I kept the books for Congress then\\
In Philadelphia town.\\
Two letters came up from the South.\\
I took their stories down.

\songpart{Verse 2 - First account, solo}
One said the Georgians crossed the line\\
And fell on Spanish ground.\\
It said they had no right to be\\
Where their bodies were found.

\songpart{Verse 3 - Second account, same melody}
The next said Spain had sent her men,\\
Five hundred to the line.\\
They lay in wait on Georgia soil\\
And fired on yours and mine.

\annotation{Refrain - Solo}\\
If Georgia strikes a match tonight,\\
The rest must bear the flame.\\
But if we leave her there to burn,\\
Can we still bear one name?

\annotation{Short fife interlude}

\songpart{Verse 4 - Solo}
Now Georgia’s gone to war with Spain.\\
Tomorrow, who can tell?\\
New York may look to Canada\\
And ring the selfsame bell.

\annotation{Refrain - Solo}\\
If Georgia strikes a match tonight,\\
The rest must bear the flame.\\
But if we leave her there to burn,\\
Can we still bear one name?

\songpart{Verse 5 - Unaccompanied}
I filed them both beneath one date\\
And left a line below.\\
There’s room enough to write who fired.\\
There’s no one here who’d know.

\annotation{Final Refrain - Unaccompanied}\\
If Georgia strikes a match tonight,\\
The rest must bear the flame.\\
But if we leave her there to burn,\\
Can we still bear one name?
\end{lyrics}
