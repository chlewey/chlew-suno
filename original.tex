\chapter{Partial Authorship}
\albumcover{covers/original/partial-authorship}
Songs Carlos Thompson wrote only in part. Most are collaborations with an AI writing on instruction around his own words -- a 1988 French poem turned into a musical-theater number by Gemini, a symphonic-metal confession split between his intro/chorus/outro and Gemini's verses and bridge, a lo-fi hip-hop track with Gemini's opening verse and Grok's later ones around his own, two \emph{Ricochets} scenes co-written with Claude, a bedroom-pop farewell with Grok's verses around his chorus, a Xumi road-trip song whose meter and rhyme Grok tightened, a \emph{Monukae} lament whose free verse Claude recast in iambic pentameter, and a multilingual \emph{Thomas' Entourage} pop song built by Claude around his Spanish chorus. One shares its lyrics with tradition instead: \emph{Pío}, his additions to the children's song \emph{Los pollitos dicen}.

\chapter{AI Mimicry}
\albumcover{covers/original/ai-mimicry}
Songs Carlos Thompson did not write at all, but that an AI wrote while trying to sound like him -- following prompts to imitate his voice and songwriting habits, or patterns drawn from his original lyrics. Gemini's two attempts look back at college as the top of the curve and rip up the page in a punk anthem; Claude's four take a liberation anthem that audits its own norms, a puppy who wants to grow up to be the grumpy cat in the window, an alternate-history legend of a Haitian general who takes Paris by treaty and by wedding, and a clear Colombian morning watched from a window.
