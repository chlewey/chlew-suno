\songtitle{A Bag Sleeping in Jamaica}

\tag*{2026}\tag{folk-rock}\tag{original-source}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by SUNO based on a short story extracted from Carlos Thompson's memoir \emph{A Life in World Events}, cowritten with Claude — a visa lapsed in 1998 and denied again in 2003, a 2009 detour through Jamaica, Queens and Staten Island on the way to visit his father in Yokohama, and an unspoken debt of kindness to Marialuz, met years earlier in a Stockholm newcomers' school. Retrieved from Dvigitt's Suno page (V6-MINI, 26 September 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Folk-rock with a funk pulse, intimate conversational male lead with clear storytelling diction, warm chorus harmonies, and a nimble rhythmic rap bridge; dry syncopated electric bass up front, tight pocket drums, bright acoustic strumming, clipped electric guitar accents, small organ figure, fuller chorus guitars; steady road-worn groove, lean verses opening into wider choruses; natural live-room warmth, close vocal, punchy low end, restrained polish.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1}
My visa wore out in ninety-eight,\\
I tried again in oh-three, got a no.\\
No solid reason, no place to be,\\
Just work that came and went too slow.\\
Then Dad called up from Yokohama,\\
Said, "Come see us, son, take the time."\\
Colombia to Japan had one hard turn:\\
Get that New York stamp, then cross the line.

\songpart{Chorus}
A bag sleeping in Jamaica,\\
Queens, in a room reserved for me.\\
While I crossed the harbor to Staten Island,\\
Past the plans I never thought I'd see.\\
One night held two old lives together:\\
My friend, my case, the miles between.\\
A bag sleeping in Jamaica,\\
While I sat where I had once been.

\songpart{Verse 2}
August, New York, near twenty-four hours,\\
A list of names I hadn't called.\\
Old classmates, voices from Conlang-L,\\
Loose threads still running through it all.\\
At last I reached Marialuz,\\
Bolivian, Stockholm, nineteen-ninety.\\
In that school of newcomers, she'd helped me\\
Leave Adriana's orbit quietly.

\annotation{Rap Bridge}\\
Nineteen years folded in a cab ride,\\
Names from the classroom turning in my mind.\\
Poland, Eritrea, Iraq, Vietnam,\\
Zaire, Angola—every desk, every dawn.\\
She never knew what that kindness had done,\\
How a long-held spell came loose, then was gone.\\
Now her Staten Island home let me stay,\\
While my suitcase checked into Queens for the day.\\
I could've told her, could've opened that door—\\
But some truths wait quiet, then travel once more.

\songpart{Verse 3}
Morning came and I caught my flight,\\
The Pacific waiting past the gate.\\
I never told her there in Stockholm;\\
I left it unsaid that night, too late.\\
The bag had kept its room till dawn,\\
I carried the rest across the sea.\\
Two ends of town, one borrowed evening,\\
And a kindness she never knew she'd leave in me.

\songpart{Final Chorus}
A bag sleeping in Jamaica,\\
Queens, in a room reserved for me.\\
While I crossed the harbor to Staten Island,\\
Past the plans I never thought I'd see.\\
One night held two old lives together:\\
My friend, my case, the miles between.\\
A bag sleeping in Jamaica,\\
And a story still folded inside me.

\songpart{Outro}
Next day, Japan.\\
The bag came back.\\
The words stayed packed.
