\songtitle{A Name Passed Down Like Iron}

\tag*{2026}\tag{chamber-pop}\tag{saulon}\tag{yomasa}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Suno based on a substory of the novel draft \emph{Saulon} by Carlos Thompson (a fictional universe of his), extracted and translated by Claude -- Jurmesdy, the Great War, and a name passed down like iron. Retrieved from Dvigitt's Suno page (V6-MINI, 12 October 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Theatrical chamber pop with a dramatic, resonant baritone lead; piano, close string quartet, and restrained low percussion establish an intimate, stately pulse. Build verses with precise, narrative phrasing and subtle orchestral tension; let choruses widen into layered strings and firm, memorable melodic lines. At the bridge, pivot sharply into dark metal with down-tuned guitars, pounding drums, and a fierce vocal edge, then return to chamber-pop restraint for the final reckoning. Cinematic dynamics, detailed lyrics-forward mix, vivid stage-like vocal performance.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1}
In twenty-five-fifteen, beneath Philippine rain,\\
Jurmesdy counted years, and chose her own refrain.\\
No suitor fit the measure Alexander had in mind;\\
A crafted Y, her image—Jhon arrived by design.\\
Muhan came two years before, the elder brother’s name;\\
Alexander stood as father, though biology would change.

\songpart{Verse 2}
The Great War took the house in a nerve-gas missile’s breath,\\
His parents, neighbors, rescuers—all swallowed up by death.\\
The bunker held its distance; the hackers struck the home.\\
Jurmesdy, fifteen, slept elsewhere, spared the poisoned room.\\
He volunteered for Uzbekistan, saw hunger, blight, disease;\\
He judged the broken world by character, not creed.

\songpart{Chorus}
A name passed down like iron, a lesson dressed as care;\\
A ledger full of futures, a balance none could bear.\\
He learned to call it mercy, he learned to call it fate—\\
But every human number still had a human face.

\songpart{Verse 3}
Five years after war, he came back to the islands,\\
Found his country wanting, found one exception shining:\\
His sister, shaped by Muhan’s faith, stood steady in the din;\\
He scorned the prayers, admired the strength they’d put within.

\songpart{Verse 4}
In twenty-five-twenty-four, nine years old, he heard\\
Jurmesdy hold each life as sacred, word by measured word.\\
Alexander said the unfit should be left to pass away;\\
Their love survived the argument. Jhon chose his uncle’s way.\\
He joined the little circles, online and close at hand,\\
And learned the risks of capital, the arithmetic of command.

\songpart{Chorus}
A name passed down like iron, a lesson dressed as care;\\
A ledger full of futures, a balance none could bear.\\
He learned to call it mercy, he learned to call it fate—\\
But every human number still had a human face.

\songpart{Bridge}
The planets filled with voices; Mars and Io could not save\\
A world that spent its children like a line item in a grave.\\
He bankrolled wars to thin the crowd, and called the columns clean;\\
The quieter the signature, the louder what it means.

\annotation{Dark Metal Bridge}\\
Count them, rank them, weigh their worth—\\
Cold hands make a colder earth.\\
No flag can wash the figures white;\\
No theorem makes a grave feel right.

\songpart{Verse 5}
Before thirty, he outgrew the banker’s borrowed chair;\\
By forty, two on Earth could match his reach and power.\\
Saulon Forecast dreamed another dream; they spoke with measured grace,\\
Their ends not wholly separate, respect taking its place.

\songpart{Final Chorus}
A name passed down like iron, a lesson dressed as care;\\
A ledger full of futures, a balance none could bear.\\
By twenty-six-twenty, the summit had his name—\\
Richest, strongest on the planet, still answerable to the flame.

\songpart{Outro}
The books lie open, every column signed.\\
A kingdom built on choices cannot leave choice behind.
\end{lyrics}
