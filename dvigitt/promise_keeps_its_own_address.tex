\songtitle{Promise Keeps Its Own Address}

\tag*{2026}\tag{musical-theater}\tag{thomas-entourage}\tag{thomas-garcia}\tag{sylvia-lai}\tag{victoria-ospina}
\begin{notes}
From the \emph{Thomas’ Entourage} narrative universe by Carlos Thompson. Based on the \emph{Victoria} vignette by Carlos Thompson (2026). Characters: Thomas Garcia, Sylvia Lai, Victoria Ospina. Lyrics by Suno.
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Hip-hop-rock musical theater, brisk  musical-forward groove around 100 BPM, live punchy drums, distorted rhythm guitar, elastic electric bass, piano-led theatrical harmonic turns, and occasional brass stabs, Verses use conversational male sing-rap with sharply articulated storytelling; Sylvia gets bright youthful melodic lines, while Victoria enters with confident athletic energy and lightly accented phrasing, Build from intimate verse snapshots into a huge ensemble-ready chorus, with call-and-response between narrator and women, a brief spoken transition, then a half-time bridge before the final triumphant reprise, Keep the mix vivid and stage-like: close dry vocals, controlled room ambience, crunchy guitars, clear lyric intelligibility, dynamic rises rather than constant loudness
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Intro}
December took her overseas,\\
A suitcase, wings, and promises.\\
Then April dropped a Telegram:\\
A birthday wrapped in exclamation marks.

\songpart{Verse 1}
“Happy anniversary!\\
And guess what!\\
I’m already eighteen!”\\
Sylvia counted every day\\
Like a clock beneath my skin.\\
Greca said, “You made a vow.”\\
Paolo said, “You know the score.”\\
I thought I’d slipped the leash somehow—\\
She was waiting at the door.

\songpart{Pre-Chorus}
Barcelona, hotel morning,\\
School year finished, suitcase calling.\\
I said, “We’ll find a place to start.”\\
She said, “You’re late. I’ve had a plan.”

\songpart{Chorus}
You can cross an ocean, change the view,\\
Make a hundred clever plans to do,\\
But a promise keeps its own address,\\
And Sylvia’s standing at the desk.\\
London, New York, Riohacha-bound,\\
Still her footsteps follow every town.\\
I thought I was the one who knew the way—\\
She had been counting down the days.

\songpart{Verse 2}
University of London,\\
Open doors and polished floors,\\
Victoria Ospina led us through\\
The future waiting past the doors.\\
PE student, Australian sound,\\
A question left politely unasked.\\
Sylvia caught her private code—\\
I couldn’t keep up with the cast.

\songpart{Verse 3}
“She’s Colombian,” Sylvia said.\\
Victoria turned, “Which part?”\\
“Bogotá,” I answered, then\\
She told us where her parents met.\\
Her family still lived there,\\
Her Spanish limped; mine limped too.\\
Our Sardinian friend just laughed—\\
At least the playing field was true.

\songpart{Bridge}
\annotation{Sylvia}\\
You like her, don’t you?
\annotation{Narrator}\\
A pretty face, a charming smile,\\
Nothing I had weighed in quite a while.\\
Then the question followed me to dinner,\\
Sat beside my plate, and made me thinner.

\annotation{Victoria}\\
We traded numbers, traded names,\\
A little spark beneath the frames.
\annotation{Narrator}\\
“Just a formality,” I said,\\
Then checked the phone beside the bed.

\annotation{Ensemble}\\
Message by message, line by line,\\
Almost flirting, never quite.\\
A door left open, a tilted sign—\\
Who knew a maybe could feel like mine?

\songpart{Final Chorus}
You can cross an ocean, change the view,\\
Make a hundred clever plans to do,\\
But a promise keeps its own address,\\
And Sylvia’s standing at the desk.\\
Sydney waiting, Victoria’s town,\\
Another flight, another countdown.\\
I asked, she said yes without delay—\\
Some stories travel where they may.

\songpart{Outro}
December flew, April replied,\\
London opened up its doors wide.\\
Barcelona, Sydney, every place—\\
A reluctant guide, a growing chase.\\
I thought I was the one who knew the way.\\
She had been counting down the days.
\end{lyrics}
