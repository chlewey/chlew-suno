\songtitle{Qi Angbu}

\tag*{2026}\tag{chamber-pop}\tag{j-pop}\tag{saulon}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Suno based on a substory of the novel draft \emph{Saulon} by Carlos Thompson (a fictional universe of his), extracted and translated by Claude -- Qi Angbu, the oldest living soul at three hundred eleven. Retrieved from Dvigitt's Suno page (V6-MINI, 12 October 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Chamber J-pop with a theatrical lyrical tenor lead, precise Japanese pop phrasing and an expressive, stage-trained vocal arc. Begin with intimate piano and pizzicato strings; let clarinet and cello answer the vocal, then build into sweeping bowed strings, taut bass, and crisp restrained pop percussion at the chorus. Use clear dynamic rises and falls, a memorable melodic refrain, dramatic pauses, close vocal presence, and a polished orchestral-pop mix with warm acoustic detail.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1}
When the collapse was named aloud,\\
Qi Angbu laughed beneath the crowded sky.\\
Three hundred eleven years in his hands,\\
A steady pulse, a lucid eye.\\
The oldest living soul, still walking,\\
Still greeting strangers by their names;\\
The future gathered at the windows,\\
While he remembered older flames.

\songpart{Pre-Chorus}
They said the body could be mended,\\
If every failing part was known.\\
But all the years they stitched together\\
Could not make any life their own.

\songpart{Chorus}
Qi Angbu, carry the years we cannot keep,\\
Past three hundred, past the waking world’s design.\\
Medicine made a longer road to travel,\\
But no road promised every heart more time.\\
When the ships depart with minds and memory,\\
Leave the tools that taught the flesh to mend;\\
Qi Angbu, at the edge of human seasons,\\
You were first—and there would be no second end.

\songpart{Verse 2}
The wealthy bought another century,\\
New organs, careful joints of steel;\\
A child in want could lose the morning\\
Before the doctors made him well.\\
A hundred twenty years, the numbers said,\\
Yet numbers thinned where famine spread;\\
War and chance and sickness kept\\
Their quiet ledger by each bed.

\songpart{Pre-Chorus}
Then every frightened prophecy\\
Found hands that turned it into harm;\\
Clinics closed, the wards went silent,\\
And faith wore violence like a charm.

\songpart{Chorus}
Qi Angbu, carry the years we cannot keep,\\
Past three hundred, past the waking world’s design.\\
Medicine made a longer road to travel,\\
But no road promised every heart more time.\\
When the ships depart with minds and memory,\\
Leave the tools that taught the flesh to mend;\\
Qi Angbu, at the edge of human seasons,\\
You were first—and there would be no second end.

\songpart{Bridge}
At three hundred eighteen, the morning broke.\\
An attack took what no illness could.\\
The first to cross three hundred years,\\
The last to stand where no one stood.\\
Twelve billion names became nine billion;\\
A planet learning how to grieve.\\
The ships kept all the knowledge written—\\
Not the machines that let us breathe.

\songpart{Final Chorus}
Qi Angbu, carry the years we cannot keep,\\
Past three hundred, past the farthest measured line.\\
Medicine gave the rich a longer season,\\
Still the end arrived in different time.\\
When the ships depart with minds and memory,\\
May the living teach the lost arts to mend;\\
Qi Angbu, at the edge of human seasons,\\
You were first—and there would be no second end.

\songpart{Outro}
Three hundred eighteen years.\\
A name the future could not keep.
\end{lyrics}
