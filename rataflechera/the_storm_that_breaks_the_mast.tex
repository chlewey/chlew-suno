\songtitle{The Storm that Breaks the Mast}

\tag*{2026}\tag{sea-shanty}\tag{folk}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Suno based on a scene and style description by Carlos Thompson -- a family ship caught in the ice, saved -- or kept -- by a drowned crew. Retrieved from rataflechera's Suno page (V6, 20 October 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Maritime folk, sea shanty.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1}
The ice came walking over the rail.\\
White wall swallowed the moon.\\
Our little ones huddled beneath the sail\\
while the mast groaned out of tune.

\songpart{Chorus}
Hold her, dead hands; hold her fast.\\
Keep the black water out.\\
We fear the storm that breaks the mast,\\
or the ghosts who sing aloud.

\songpart{Verse 2}
The captain’s coat swung empty and slow,\\
though no one stood by the door.\\
Then pale hands hauled at the bucking bow\\
and drove us clear of the floe.

\songpart{Chorus}
Hold her, dead hands; hold her fast.\\
Keep the black water out.\\
We fear the storm that breaks the mast\\
or the ghosts who sing aloud.

\songpart{Verse 3}
“Are they saving us?” my brother cried.\\
“Are they keeping us here?” said my wife.\\
The children watched as the dead men tried\\
to stitch the hull back to life.

\songpart{Breakdown}
The gale leaned hard till the rigging screamed.\\
The deck split under our feet.\\
Those drowned men worked where the lanterns gleamed,\\
with salt in their hollow cheeks.

\songpart{Final Chorus}
Hold her, dead hands; hold her fast.\\
The dawn is a blade of blue.\\
We fear the storm that breaks the mast\\
and what the dead might ask us to do.

\songpart{Outro}
When morning found us beyond the ice,\\
no ghost remained inside—\\
only a wet handprint, cold as the sea,\\
pressed on the cabin light.
\end{lyrics}
