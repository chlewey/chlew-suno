\songtitle{The Emperor’s New Clothes}

\tag*{2014}\tag{folk-rock}\tag{original-source}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Suno based on a 2014 blog post written by Carlos Thompson under the pen name Rataflechera.
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Upbeat folk-rock jingle, brisk acoustic-guitar strumming, handclaps, stomping kick and snare, bright electric-guitar hooks, warm bass, playful major-key verses with a lightly skeptical edge, conversational lead vocal, tight harmony responses in the chorus, compact radio-friendly arrangement with a short instrumental fiddle break, clean punchy mix and an unmistakable singalong hook
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1}
When an argument sounds absurd,\\
Don’t throw it out on the first word.\\
Was it twisted for a cheap reply?\\
A straw-man suit in a tailor’s lie?

If it’s the claim in its original frame,\\
Two doors open, and they’re not the same:\\
Maybe the thought is foolish through and through,\\
Or maybe the missing fool is you.

\songpart{Chorus}
Who’s wearing the emperor’s new clothes?\\
Who sees the stitch, who only supposes?\\
Say what you see, but check what you know—\\
The crown may be bare, or your eyes may be closed.\\
Who’s wearing the emperor’s new clothes?

\songpart{Verse 2}
The emperor marched in a priceless thread,\\
While every courtier nodded their head.\\
“Only a fool cannot see this gown!”\\
So silence dressed the whole town down.

Then a child with a plain-eyed view\\
Said, “There’s nothing hanging on you.”\\
A ridiculous claim can rule by fear\\
When nobody wants to look unclear.

\songpart{Chorus}
Who’s wearing the emperor’s new clothes?\\
Who sees the stitch, who only supposes?\\
Say what you see, but check what you know—\\
The crown may be bare, or your eyes may be closed.\\
Who’s wearing the emperor’s new clothes?

\songpart{Verse 3}
Particles aren’t marbles rolling in space,\\
We’re fish-clade cousins in the family place.\\
Time and matter once burned bright and small,\\
Thirteen-point-eight billion years before it all.

It sounds like a joke when the old maps bend,\\
But a working machine can answer back again.\\
When strange ideas build the tools we use,\\
Maybe the strange part is what we refuse.

\songpart{Verse 4}
A three-in-one God with an endless mind,\\
Everywhere present, yet hard to find.\\
Grace and works and a burning end—\\
Questions we’re warned not to ask as friends.

From the outside, the story may seem\\
Like a royal robe stitched out of dream.\\
But mystery can claim what proof can’t show;\\
The question is where the evidence goes.

\songpart{Bridge}
Some afterlife claims sit beyond the test,\\
Leave them aside with the rest of the rest.\\
But when the crops fail, when the engines stall,\\
We trust the methods that answer the call.

By their fruits, you can know the tree:\\
One makes a bridge, one silences me.\\
If a doctrine needs threats to guard its throne,\\
That’s a warning written in stone.

\songpart{Final Chorus}
Who’s wearing the emperor’s new clothes?\\
Who sees the stitch, who only supposes?\\
Science may sound strange, but its bridges hold;\\
A dogma that breaks them is growing old.\\
Who’s wearing the emperor’s new clothes?

\songpart{Outro}
If science sounds ridiculous, learn a little more.\\
If religion sounds ridiculous, ask what it’s for.\\
Check the claim, then check your eyes—\\
The barest truth needs no disguise.
\end{lyrics}
