\songtitle{No Cartagena Square}

\tag*{2026}\tag{folk-rock}\label{sec:no-cartagena-square}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Claude from a seed by Carlos Thompson -- the siege of Cartagena de Indias in 1741: Vernon's fleet, the fort of San Felipe de Barajas, Blas de Lezo, the fever, and a London that names its streets for what it won. Retrieved from rataflechera's Suno page (V6, 7 November 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Folk-rock sea ballad, stacked chorus overdubs and gang vocals widen the refrain; strummed acoustic guitar, sustained fiddle and rising snare rolls carry a measured 6/8 lilt; dry baritone verses give way to a spoken outro as the instruments fall away.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1}
Before the sands of Normandy\\
no fleet like this had gone:\\
a hundred eighty ships or so,\\
all Britain could have thrown.\\
It halted at a little fort\\
that guarded the bay's door,\\
and scurvy, flux, and fever then\\
began to keep the score.

\songpart{Chorus}
London has a Trafalgar Square.\\
London has a Waterloo Place,\\
a Portobello Road, for a town\\
that Vernon took, two years before.\\
It has no Cartagena Square.\\
You name your streets for what you won,\\
and no one ever asked.

\songpart{Verse 2}
The little castle fell in March.\\
A dispatch crossed the sea,\\
and London read the city's won\\
and cheered, and called for tea.\\
But Lezo walked inside the walls\\
with what was left of men,\\
and on the hill Barajas stood\\
and waited for them then.

\songpart{Verse 3}
They climbed Barajas in the dark\\
on ladders, some too short,\\
and Spanish guns, already aimed,\\
were firing from the fort.\\
The rains came in. The wards ran full.\\
Each morning, count the dead:\\
the fever took what guns had not,\\
and nothing more was said.

\songpart{Chorus}
London has a Trafalgar Square.\\
London has a Waterloo Place,\\
a Portobello Road, for a town\\
that Vernon took, two years before.\\
It has no Cartagena Square.\\
You name your streets for what you took,\\
and no one ever said.

\songpart{Bridge}
Old Grog knew how to stop scurvy,\\
but wasn't ready then;\\
a war began for one man's ear,\\
and cost so many men.\\
Lezo, one eye, one arm, one leg,\\
held off the English fleet;\\
Vernon and Wentworth could not take\\
the walls, and chose retreat.

\songpart{Verse 4}
The fleet turned back to Jamaica\\
in May, with half its crews.\\
They say a medal had been struck\\
to celebrate the news.\\
Lezo would not outlive the year;\\
the fever took him too.\\
The medal may be myth. The war\\
went on, as wars will do.

\songpart{Final Chorus}
London has a Trafalgar Square.\\
London has a Waterloo Place,\\
a Portobello Road, for a town\\
that Vernon took, two years before.\\
It has no Cartagena Square.\\
You name your streets for what you keep.\\
Cartagena kept the hill.

\songpart{Outro, spoken, instruments out}
«God damn you, Lezo!» Vernon said.\\
Historians doubt he said it.\\
It's repeated anyway.
\end{lyrics}
