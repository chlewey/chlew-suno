\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Claude based on \emph{Ricochets} by Carlos Thompson -- chapter 14 (\emph{Agent Perez}): Carlson's interior voice in the DEA conference room in Brickell, when the debriefing breaks its script with the question of the title, and his lack of reaction becomes a reaction. Retrieved from rataflechera's Suno page (V6, 24 November 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Hip-hop boom-bap, low, flat, controlled male baritone spoken close to the mic; roomy, unprocessed, almost inflectionless verses, with half-spoken talk-sing shaping the sung murmur only on hooks. Sparse minimal production: dry drums and deep sub bass leave hook beats out, while the verses return at half-time; 84 BPM, D minor. Dry boom-bap kick and rimshot, a detuned Rhodes loop, air-conditioning hum, ticking wall clock, isolated pen clicks, and a clock-and-sub bridge.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Intro}
\annotation{air-conditioning hum, a wall clock, a pen clicking once}

\songpart{Verse 1}
Glass table, river under it, gray as a file,\\
the bridge lifting for a boat that isn't in a hurry.\\
Coffee in a paper cup going cold by design,\\
somebody's notes upside down where I can read them.\\
Same questions as last time, same order, same weight,\\
I answer the way you set down something heavy:\\
slowly, both hands, nothing dropped.\\
Dates. Names. Who drove. Who paid.\\
It's a script. I know the script.\\
I helped write scripts like this.

\annotation{Hook}
\annotation{beat drops out}\\
Didn't you learn your lesson?\\
It lands in the room like a cup knocked over,\\
and nobody reaches for a napkin.\\
Didn't you learn your lesson?

\songpart{Verse 2}
\annotation{beat returns, half-time}\\
Somewhere in the middle the script runs out of paper,\\
and the question that comes isn't on the page.\\
It names the garage. It names the little shoes.\\
It names what I did and what I didn't do,\\
and puts it on the table like the coffee,\\
like it's mine to drink.\\
I keep my hands flat on the glass.\\
I count the bridge going down.\\
I look at the river and the river looks like nothing.\\
My face does what I trained it to do,\\
which is nothing,\\
which is everything.

\annotation{Hook}
\annotation{beat drops out}\\

\songpart{Bridge}
\annotation{only the sub bass and the clock}\\
Here's what I learned:\\
a still face is a sentence too.\\
A man who doesn't flinch\\
has already told you where it hurts.\\
The quiet I'm holding is a confession,\\
signed, dated, in the margin of your notes,\\
and I can watch you read it,\\
and I can't take it back.

\songpart{Verse 3}
They'll write that the subject showed no reaction.\\
They'll be right, and they'll be wrong.\\
The pen goes on. The clock goes on.\\
The boat clears the bridge and the bridge comes down.\\
I could answer. I've got answers.\\
Most of them are true.\\
None of them are the lesson.

\annotation{Hook}
\annotation{Rhodes alone, sung murmur}\\
Didn't you learn your lesson?\\
I learned it.\\
I learned it in April.\\
I'm still learning it.

\songpart{Outro}
\annotation{air-conditioning hum, the clock, a chair rolling back}\\
Next question.
\annotation{End}
