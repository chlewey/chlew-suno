\songtitle{Ask Someone with the Skill}
\tag*{2026}\tag{folk-rock}\tag{quora-posting}\label{sec:ask-someone-with-the-skill}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Claude mimicking the style of Carlos Thompson, based on a 2026 Quora post by him -- a scientist asked at lunch whether God can be ruled out: define it so a test can fail or succeed, or leave it to metaphysics. Retrieved from Carlos Th's Suno page (V6-MINI, 9 November 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Folk-rock, roomy plate reverb around fingerpicked acoustic guitar and brushed snare, handclaps on the chorus and a harmonica line in the bridge; steady 96 BPM, deadpan dry baritone, hushed and close-mic with gang-vocal shouts on the hook.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
[Verse 1]
A scientist at lunch is asked
if God can be ruled out.
The question cannot be answered
as is. She has her doubts.

[Pre-Chorus]
God is no concept science owns
unless you make it one.
Ignoring it is rather sound;
ruling it out, not done.

[Chorus]
Define it so the test can fail
and so it can succeed:
give it effects we can measure
and numbers we can read.

[Verse 2]
Don't cheat the definition now:
the doubter's doomed to fail,
the believer's tautology,
a proof that tells no tale.

[Pre-Chorus]
The same goes for the other side:
to prove it takes a frame.
Without a way to measure it
both proof and doubt the same.

[Chorus]
Define it so the test can fail
and so it can succeed:
one definition, signed by both,
of matter, guaranteed.

[Bridge]
And if no matter can be found,
don't hand the lab the bill;
it turns to metaphysics then,
ask someone with the skill.

[Final Chorus]
Define it so the test can fail
and so it can succeed;
and if no definition comes,
ignore it, as agreed.
\end{lyrics}

\songtitle{The Town Was Gone}

\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Suno based on a Claude-produced excerpt of \emph{A Life in World Events}, the memoir of Carlos Thompson -- November 1985, at twelve: the Palace of Justice in flames, then the radio news of the Nevado del Ruiz eruption, and the pilot who found no Armero where the town should be. Retrieved from Carlos Th's Suno page (V6-MINI, 12 November 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Blues/soul ballad, slow 6/8 sway with a restrained pocket; intimate male baritone, close-miked and weathered, clear narrative phrasing with controlled rises rather than belting. Begin with sparse piano and muted electric guitar; bring in warm organ and brushed drums beneath the first verse, then deepen the low end and add restrained backing harmonies as the account turns from human violence to the vanished town. Let the chorus open wider but stay solemn; strip back to piano and voice for the final image, ending with a quiet unresolved chord. Warm analog room character, spacious vocal, natural dynamics, no melodrama.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
[Verse 1]
November had already bent the morning low,
I was twelve, watching smoke where the news lights showed.

Men made ruin, and the radio named names.

[Verse 2]

My alarm clock speaking in its measured style.
Ruiz had opened; first the numbers came,
A few dozen lost, spread across the names.

[Chorus]

Twenty-two thousand lives, and no lights to see.

[Final Chorus]

[Outro]
