\part{Ska, Trap \& Funk}
Three small, distinct genres, each in its own album.

\chapter{Ska}
\albumcover{covers/pop/ska}
Ska is the Jamaican genre that predates reggae, born in the late 1950s out of mento and calypso crossed with American jazz and R\&B: upbeat tempos, walking basslines, horn sections, and the trademark offbeat "skank" strum on the guitar or keys. A genre grab-bag rather than a single story: a private-jet travelogue through the Thomas' Entourage universe with Victoria, Claire, Sylvia, and Pilar in tow, and a Spanish-language family portrait of Miguel Roa from \emph{Ricochets}. Two songs carry no plot at all -- an ode to a windowseat reader and a roll call of ten actual cats. \emph{Bogotá Backbone} closes it out as a leftover from a 2000 smart-city proposal, recast as an anthem for civic infrastructure.

\chapter{Trap}
\albumcover{covers/pop/trap}
Trap grew out of early-2000s Southern hip-hop: rapid triplet hi-hat rolls, deep sub-bass 808s, sparse dark synths, and a slower, heavier low end than mainstream rap. Mostly world-building rather than narrative: two entries in the geography-cycles series trace river systems and trade routes across East and Southeast Asia, while \emph{Teal Tips on the Bed} is a plain unspoken-crush vignette. \emph{Temblando} revives a 2010 Enwigh Peady confession of compulsion and self-loss, and \emph{Five Rooms, One Key} folds five earlier songs about buried truths and institutional damage into one trap track about what stays locked away.

\chapter{Funk}
\albumcover{covers/pop/funk}
Funk split off from soul and R\&B in the mid-1960s, built around syncopated basslines, tight rhythm-guitar "chicken scratch," punchy horn stabs, and a relentless groove anchored on the downbeat. Two character studies from \emph{Ricochets} anchor the album: Henry Little, an FBI agent whose gift for reading people curdles into something more compromised, and Khamid Khaled, the Marine-trained bodyguard who protects before the danger shows. \emph{Actual and Relevant} is a funk sermon built from a Quora argument about evolution, deism, and doubt, while \emph{What Remains Where We Are} reworks the same buried-truths material as \emph{Five Rooms, One Key} into a funk arrangement. \emph{Beachside Boogie} rounds it out as a purely instrumental groove.

\part{Regional Pop}
Pop with a strong regional signature: the vispop cycle and the K-pop/J-pop cluster.

\chapter{Vispop}
\albumcover{covers/pop/vispop}
Vispop fuses the Swedish \emph{visa} tradition -- intimate, lyric-driven troubadour song, acoustic and melodic -- with modern pop production, usually sung in Swedish. A near-complete portrait of Erika Czajka from the Thomas' Entourage universe: her Stockholm-to-Utrecht upbringing and botany career, her careful philosophy about naming relationships, the Barcelona night she met Thomas, and her measured place among Natalia, Gabi, and Sylvia inside his orbit. \emph{Luciafestens dugg} retells \emph{That Night} in Swedish. The lone outsider is \emph{Skälvning}, an electronic vispop re-genre of a 2010 Enwigh Peady nu-metal confession, unconnected to Erika's story.

\chapter{K-pop \& J-pop}
\albumcover{covers/pop/k-pop-and-j-pop}
K-pop and J-pop are South Korea's and Japan's mainstream pop industries respectively -- both prized for glossy, maximalist production and hook-dense choruses, with K-pop leaning harder into its idol-group choreography and vocal-blend aesthetic; here the labels mark production and vocal style rather than any literal idol-group context. Six songs build out Natalia Kyou Park's story: her aerospace career and trilingual Korean-Japanese-American upbringing, the Milan hotel-bar meeting with Thomas, her working partnership and friction with Erika, and her own declaration of independence in \emph{Easy to Breath}. \emph{Chiang Mai to Bangkok} is a looser travel diary that still brushes past Thomas, Gabi, and Sylvia, while \emph{Draw the Tree} is a Quora-sourced argument against credulity and pseudoscience. \emph{Ack-Rostica} and \emph{Awk-Rustica} are the odd pair out, two Spanish acrostic poems from 2010 under the Enwigh Peady persona.

\part{Mainstream Pop}

\chapter{Symphonic \& Chamber Pop}
\albumcover{covers/pop/symphonic-and-chamber-pop}
Two related, classical-adjacent styles share this album: symphonic pop wraps pop songwriting in a full orchestral arrangement -- strings, brass, choir -- for cinematic scale, while chamber pop keeps the same instinct more intimate, scored for a small classical ensemble instead of a full orchestra. A mix of gothic AI-generated portraits (\emph{Parapet of Ash}, \emph{Amber on the Cliff}, \emph{Copperfire Crown}) sits alongside two invented-language pieces, \emph{dij Schdzjarvk} and a conlang setting of the Lord's Prayer in \emph{O ku pater de nos}. \emph{Guiltless Flame} is a motel-room affair from the Encounters arc between Felicia Campbell and Gabriel Moreno, while \emph{Purity Test} follows Father Peter navigating a conservative purity test alongside Pastor Miguel Jara in the fictional town of Piravita. \emph{A la distancia} is a Spanish confession of secret, unrequited longing observed from afar, and \emph{Frost in Gold} is a plain winter-joy anthem with no story attached.

\chapter{Indie-Pop}
\albumcover{covers/pop/indie-pop}
Indie-pop keeps pop's melodic instincts but swaps major-label gloss for a guitar-driven, DIY-rooted production ethos -- understated, sometimes deliberately rough around the edges, favoring intimacy over polish. Mostly portraits and vignettes with no throughline: three AI-generated character studies (\emph{Zelma in the Mist}, \emph{Terracotta View}, \emph{Golden Hour Terrace}), an instrumental study of strangers in a hallway, and \emph{Food Truck Line} watching two people share an unspoken moment from the Encounters arc. \emph{Todos al mismo lado} adapts a political cartoon into a Spanish critique of blind consensus, while \emph{Signed by Don Addis} is a shaggy-dog story about hunting down a half-remembered comic strip's credit. \emph{Stop or Ellipsis} turns a generational text-punctuation argument into a co-written bit, \emph{Domestic Realism} sets a Bogotá office routine to Spanish synth-pop, and \emph{Si pienso en ti} revives a 1990 poem of unshakeable longing.

\part{Alternative Pop}

\chapter{Dream-Pop}
\albumcover{covers/pop/dream-pop}
Dream-pop is an atmospheric alternative style built on washes of reverb and delay, blurred or shimmering guitar and synth textures, and breathy, often half-buried vocals -- mood and texture placed ahead of the hook. Two parallel character arcs dominate: Diana Naji's restless independence and her brief Casablanca-then-Ibiza history with Thomas (\emph{Diana Moves Cleanly}, \emph{Le Cordon Bleu}, \emph{Let It Stay Sweet}, \emph{The Weight of Gravity}), and Liis Sepp's clear-eyed, unsentimental fling with him in Tallinn, told in both English and Estonian (\emph{Indigo Portrait}, \emph{Uks, mis pole lahti}, \emph{Unopened Door}). \emph{The Motion and the Spark} and \emph{Right Here} follow Sylvia Lai's growth and her guarded friendship with Gabi over a staged photo shoot. \emph{Moonstone Veil} is an unrelated AI-generated portrait, and \emph{Entre cajas y llamadas} is a Spanish plea from a family losing touch while selling off an apartment.

\chapter{Sophisti-Pop}
\albumcover{covers/pop/sophisti-pop}
Sophisti-pop is the polished, jazz- and soul-inflected style that came out of 1980s Britain (Sade, Prefab Sprout, The Style Council): smooth production, sophisticated chord voicings, and an urbane, adult sensibility a step removed from ordinary pop. Thomas' Entourage supplies most of the set: Aurora Swansen gets a femme-fatale portrait, and \emph{Moon at the Cabin} and \emph{My Delicate Verse} both turn on Thomas' bond with Gabi -- the first with Claire Jones present as press scrutiny closes in, the second Thomas' own accounting of Gabi as partner, wingman, and unclassifiable delicate verse. \emph{Steady Hands} steps outside that universe into \emph{Ricochets} for a portrait of flight attendant Felicia's unshakeable composure, and \emph{Ghost Cheek Kiss} closes the album with Fran's guilt-laced infidelity from the short story \emph{Drift}.

\chapter{Misc}
\albumcover{covers/pop/misc}
This album exists for pop's leftover subgenres -- styles distinct enough to name but without enough songs here to fill an album of their own: art-pop (idiosyncratic structures and production borrowed from art-rock), bedroom-pop (lo-fi, home-recorded intimacy), singer-songwriter (voice and instrument foregrounded ahead of production), alt-pop (pop hooks filtered through an indie or alternative sensibility), sunshine-pop (bright, layered vocal harmonies in a 1960s Californian mold), synth-pop (synthesizer-led, drum-machine pulse), cosmopolitan-pop (polished, well-traveled, often multilingual pop), epic-pop (widescreen, anthemic builds), adult-contemporary (smooth, radio-friendly, mid-tempo), and lo-fi (deliberately rough, low-fidelity production).
