[Spoken intro]

about four hundred and fifty miles.
Everything on it is optional.

[Verse 1]

He takes the boat to the base, stands in the mist,
gets wet to the knees and calls it a plan.

[Chorus]
The road runs east and he lets it,
one exit, one town, one coffee at a time.
Not every exit is worth the stop.
Some of them are.

[Verse 2]
Rochester has a museum devoted to play
and a park that goes purple with lilacs in May.

He reads every sign. He is the only one.

[Chorus]
The road runs east and he lets it,
one exit, one town, one coffee at a time.
Not every town is worth the stop.
Some of them are.

[Verse 3]
Utica he can't explain:
a road race named for boilermakers,
an arts institute with a better collection than it needs,
and a plate of chicken riggies he orders for the story.
Albany's capitol took thirty years to build.
He respects that. He climbs the stairs slowly.

[Bridge, spoken]
Case one: on the way. Rochester, Syracuse, Utica, Albany, Springfield.
Case two: a detour that pays. Niagara, for the water.
Saratoga, for the springs and the track.
Lake George, for a fort from 1755 and a lake too cold to argue with.
Case three: a detour for the sake of it. Hartford.
He goes anyway.

[Verse 4]
Springfield keeps the basketball hall of fame
and a garden of bronze for a man named Seuss, who was born there.
In Hartford a man who wrote about rivers
kept a house with more rooms than a family needs.
He pays for the tour. He asks one question. Nobody knows.

[Chorus]
The road runs east and he lets it,
one exit, one town, one coffee at a time.
Not every detour is worth the stop.
Some of them are.

[Verse 5]
Boston is last and the least surprising.
A red brick line on the sidewalk tells him where to go.
It is the only thing on the trip that does.
He follows it down to the harbor and the tea ships
and sits there until the light changes.

[Final Chorus]
The road ran east and he let it,
one exit, one town, one coffee at a time.
Not every mile was worth the stop.
Most of them were.
