\songtitle{Static on the Radio}
\setin{1978/1989}

\tag*{2026}\tag{folk-rock}\tag{autobiographical}\label{sec:static-on-the-radio}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Claude based on \emph{A Life in World Events}, the memoir of Carlos Thompson -- a Bogotá childhood and adolescence: the first school day, Sundays among the orange trees, tenth-grade crushes said all wrong, and the news jingle on the radio, including the November morning a town was gone. Retrieved from Carlos Th's Suno page (V6-MINI, 9 November 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Folk-Rock, tape saturation warms an acoustic strum that builds to full band on the chorus; electric guitar, bass and steady drums fill out the mid-tempo groove in the key of G; husky, smoky male vocals climb through a wide range into top notes, then turn half-spoken at the bridge.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1}
Lost my plane on the first school day,\\
Hens in the yard, I ran away.\\
Sundays down to the orange trees,\\
Sister laughing, summer breeze.\\
Radio set for the morning bell,\\
Numbers in my head, stories to tell.\\
Math and a borrowed tie,\\
Everything bigger than I knew why.

\songpart{Pre-Chorus}
Then the jingle plays,\\
We turn it down and go on with our days.

\songpart{Chorus}
Oh, Bogotá, I was twelve, I was fifteen,\\
Love on my mind, the news in between.\\
Turn it down low, let the guitar sing,\\
Static on the radio, remembering.

\songpart{Verse 2}
Tenth grade, and there’s a girl in class,\\
I wrote it down, I let it pass.\\
When I spoke, I got it wrong,\\
Heart like a bad radio song.\\
Cousins, crushes, neighbors too,\\
Every girl was someone new.\\
One the one, the rest in line,\\
Nothing ever quite on time.

\songpart{Pre-Chorus}
Then the jingle plays,\\
We turn it down and go on with our days.

\songpart{Chorus}
Oh, Bogotá, I was shy, I was fifteen,\\
Said it all wrong, the saddest I’d been.\\
Turn it down low, let the guitar sing,\\
Static on the radio, everything.

\songpart{Verse 3}
November morning, radio on,\\
A town was there, and then was gone.\\
We went to school and did our sums,\\
Nobody said where the quiet comes.\\
Now the years have rearranged the street,\\
But the jingle still keeps the beat.\\
Somewhere a boy is holding on,\\
Thinking of a girl till the dawn.

\songpart{Bridge - half spoken}
We learned to listen, learned to look away,\\
Not from the pain, just to get through the day

\songpart{Final Chorus}
Oh, Bogotá, we were young, we were here,\\
Love up close and the rest far, not clear.\\
Turn it down low, let the guitar sing,\\
Static on the radio, remembering.
\end{lyrics}
