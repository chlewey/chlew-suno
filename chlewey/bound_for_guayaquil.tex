\songtitle{Bound for Guayaquil}

\tag*{2026}\tag{synth-pop}\tag{autobiographical}\label{sec:bound-for-guayaquil}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Suno based on a Claude-produced excerpt of \emph{A Life in World Events}, the memoir of Carlos Thompson -- 11 September 2001: the news on La W and CNN, Usenet's alt.history.what-if, and two days later a flight to a new job at the World Trade Center of Guayaquil. Retrieved from Carlos Th's Suno page (V6-MINI, 13 November 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Reflective synth-pop in D minor at 112 BPM, steady restrained electronic pulse with rounded synth bass, crisp dry kick and snare, glassy arpeggiated keys, and muted guitar accents. Intimate male lead vocal, clear conversational phrasing that opens into a memorable melodic chorus. Build gradually from sparse, close-up verses to a wider layered chorus; add a brief tense instrumental lift before the final chorus, then resolve into a quiet, unresolved-feeling synth outro. Polished but understated production, focused vocal, cinematic dynamics.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1}
I was tying my shoes when La W broke through,\\
A plane had struck a tower in New York.\\
We turned the television; CNN was still guessing,\\
The picture held its breath, then cut away.\\
I saw the second plane coming in,\\
Then the studio swallowed the view.\\
An accident could no longer hold;\\
The morning changed its name.

\songpart{Chorus}
And I was bound for Guayaquil,\\
With two days left to understand.\\
A voice through dial-up, a job I’d never done,\\
And I was sent there as the man.\\
The World Trade Center, Guayaquil—\\
Just a building, people said.\\
Still, I packed beneath that impossible coincidence,\\
With the news inside my head.

\songpart{Verse 2}
I drove to work with the radio on,\\
Four confirmed, four out of reach.\\
Every flight across the country grounded,\\
Every headline out ahead of speech.\\
At the office, nothing would begin;\\
I opened Usenet, alt.history.what-if.\\
I trusted those far-flung minds to see\\
What the television couldn’t give.

\songpart{Pre-Chorus}
They knew the maps, the old disputes,\\
The angles networks couldn’t fit.\\
One voice named Al-Qaeda from the start;\\
The rest of us were guessing at it.

\songpart{Chorus}
And I was bound for Guayaquil,\\
With two days left to understand.\\
A voice through dial-up, a job I’d never done,\\
And I was sent there as the man.\\
The World Trade Center, Guayaquil—\\
Just a building, people said.\\
Still, I packed beneath that impossible coincidence,\\
With the news inside my head.

\songpart{Bridge}
Many cities have a tower with that name.\\
I knew that. I knew it wasn’t fate.\\
But names can catch on passing thoughts\\
When the world has tilted out of place.\\
I folded shirts, checked every cable,\\
Tried to keep the workday plain.\\
Two days on, I’d cross an ocean\\
With that morning on the plane.

\songpart{Final Chorus}
I was bound for Guayaquil,\\
With a case and borrowed confidence.\\
A voice through dial-up, distant hands,\\
A new system and a deadline in the distance.\\
The World Trade Center, Guayaquil—\\
An ordinary name, a separate place.\\
Still I carried that strange conjunction,\\
And the faces from the broadcast stayed.

\songpart{Outro}
La W in the morning.\\
The television cut away.\\
Guayaquil, two days later.\\
A name I couldn’t quite misplace.
\end{lyrics}
