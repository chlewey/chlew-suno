\songtitle{October Calls}
\setin{2013-10}

\tag*{2026}\tag{country}\tag{autobiographical}\label{sec:october-calls}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Suno based on a Claude-produced excerpt of \emph{A Life in World Events}, the memoir of Carlos Thompson -- an October in Bogotá: a day with Jes from Stockholm, then two calls, Luzby's mother saying goodbye in hospital and, near the month's end, the death of Abuelita Ana, already mourned before the news. Retrieved from Carlos Th's Suno page (V6-MINI, 13 November 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Reflective country-soul in D minor at 124 BPM, steady restrained 4/4 pocket with a laid-back half-time feel, warm male baritone with close conversational phrasing and controlled emotional lift. Begin with muted piano and dry acoustic guitar; bring in brushed drums, rounded bass, and subtle organ as the story opens up. Keep the first verse observational and sunlit, then shift into a darker, spacious refrain as the first call arrives; use a brief instrumental breath between the two October calls, then let the final verse settle into quiet acceptance. Soulful but understated vocal runs only at emotional peaks, light harmony on the closing refrain, organic room sound, clear lyric-forward mix, no melodramatic swell.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1}
October opened clear in Bogotá,\\
Jes came down from Stockholm for a day.\\
We walked the old stone streets together,\\
La Puerta Falsa, Monserrate on the way.\\
The city held its history around us,\\
And for a while the hours felt like home.\\
Then evening found me turning for the driveway,\\
When Luzby called, afraid to be alone.

\songpart{Chorus}
Two calls in October, two goodbyes already near,\\
One night to say the words that mattered, one morning to hear.\\
No thunder split the window, no answer came from above,\\
Just a life letting go in the slow, hard shape of love.

\songpart{Verse 2}
Her mother lived with us for years,\\
Till memory took the rooms away.\\
We found a place that could keep watch,\\
Then came the hospital, the scans, the day\\
They said two masses, risks too great,\\
And I still wonder what a scan might change.\\
I drove Luzby there on the first of October;\\
She said goodbye while the world stayed strange.

\songpart{Chorus}
Two calls in October, two goodbyes already near,\\
One night to say the words that mattered, one morning to hear.\\
No thunder split the window, no answer came from above,\\
Just a life letting go in the slow, hard shape of love.

\songpart{Verse 3}
Near month’s end, out in Facatativá,\\
Working with Oruga Amarilla that day,\\
They told me Abuelita Ana had died;\\
Her body had been leaving in its way.\\
At my last visit, something in me knew\\
She’d gone before the final breath was through.\\
I searched for grief and found a quiet fact:\\
I’d said goodbye before the news came back.

\songpart{Bridge}
Was it fate, or stoicism,\\
Or a faith I’d only just set down?\\
I can’t name the thing that held me steady,\\
Only that the care was still around.

\songpart{Final Chorus}
Two calls in October, two goodbyes already near,\\
One night to say the words that mattered, one morning to hear.\\
It mattered, though the grief came softly, though no answer came from above,\\
Sometimes death is not the leaving—just the last page of a love.

\songpart{Outro}
October closed without a verdict,\\
Only names I carry, still and true.
\end{lyrics}
