\songtitle{On the Road to Chachagüí}

% -> personal.tex (Biographical); stories.tex (Self); generos.tex (Ballads & Bare Folk)
\tag*{2026}\tag{folk-rock}\tag{original-source}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by SUNO based on a short story extracted from Carlos Thompson's memoir \emph{A Life in World Events}, cowritten with Claude -- January 1997, Carnaval de Negros y Blancos in Pasto, Laguna de Cumbal, and a first kiss at twenty-four with the unnamed girl on his right shoulder, not with la Mona on his left. Retrieved from Carlos Th's Suno page (V6-MINI, 26 September 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
road folk-rock, South American folk rock, driving acoustic guitar rhythm, warm male vocals, storytelling indie folk, quena flute, requinto guitar, charango, bombo legüero percussion, dynamic Capishca and Carnavalito Andean folk rhythm sections
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Verse 1}
January twilight on the southern line\\
An empty highway lost in ninety-seven time\\
Luz Elena at the window, dust upon the glass\\
Fourteen hours south before the borders pass\\
A house in Pasto open wide to every stray\\
Carnaval de Negros y Blancos underway

\songpart{Chorus}
On the road to Chachagüí, shoulder to shoulder\\
Left arm around la Mona, right hand holding her\\
The engine hummed a rhythm through the winding floor\\
I had no script for what my lips were waiting for

\annotation{Capishca Bridge}
\annotation{Instrumental: energetic Capishca rhythm, fast 6/8 strumming, quena lead melody, charango, requinto solo}

\songpart{Verse 2}
Cumbal lake at sunset, tents pitched in the chill\\
Sleeping through the dark until the clock stood still\\
Surreal parenthesis, midnight cookfire sparks\\
Volcano looming quiet through the cold and dark\\
Then the bus to Chachagüí in the afternoon light\\
Two heads resting heavy, two lives held tight

\songpart{Chorus}
On the road to Chachagüí, shoulder to shoulder\\
Left arm around la Mona, right hand holding her\\
Her mouth came close to mine, slightly parted there\\
I learned the grammar in the mountain air

\annotation{Carnavalito Bridge}
\annotation{Instrumental: driving Carnavalito rhythm, bombo legüero, syncopated panpipes, quena counter-melody, acoustic guitar drive}

\songpart{Verse 3}
My first real kiss at twenty-four years old\\
Passionate and sudden in the high-altitude cold\\
While one quiet part of me stayed sitting there\\
Feeling la Mona's breathing in her golden hair\\
The lake remains, the road, the sunset sleep\\
A girl whose name I am still trying to keep

\songpart{Outro}
I remember the kiss, I remember the lake\\
I remember the turn the southern highways take\\
A girl whose name I am still trying to keep
\annotation{Fade out with quena and requinto over acoustic strum}
\end{lyrics}
