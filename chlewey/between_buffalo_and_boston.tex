\songtitle{Between Buffalo and Boston}

\tag*{2026}\tag{folk-rock}\tag{road-folk}\tag{ai-based}\tag{roads}
\begin{notes}
Lyrics by Claude, written in an attempt to mimic Carlos Thompson's voice and songwriting style, based on a 2023 ChatGPT answer to Carlos Thompson's request for road-trip advice -- the stops, detours worth taking and detours for their own sake between Buffalo and Boston. Retrieved from Carlos Th's Suno page (V6-MINI, 26 October 2026).
\end{notes}
\begin{style}
Road folk-rock; gravelly baritone with clipped, rhythmic staccato phrasing, spoken intro and spoken bridge, gang vocals with soft harmony pads beneath the chorus; strummed acoustic guitar, driving kick drum, harmonica lead and answering slide guitar; steady 100 BPM shuffle; close-miked vocal and guitar, drums dropping out for the spoken outro.
\end{style}
\begin{lyrics}
\songpart{Spoken intro}
There is a road from Buffalo to Boston,\\
about four hundred and fifty miles.\\
Everything on it is optional.

\songpart{Verse 1}
He leaves Buffalo with a full tank and no schedule.\\
The falls are half an hour north. He goes anyway.\\
He takes the boat to the base, stands in the mist,\\
gets wet to the knees and calls it a plan.

\songpart{Chorus}
The road runs east and he lets it,\\
one exit, one town, one coffee at a time.\\
Not every exit is worth the stop.\\
Some of them are.

\songpart{Verse 2}
Rochester has a museum devoted to play\\
and a park that goes purple with lilacs in May.\\
He spends an afternoon at the Strong,\\
feels ten years old, and tells no one.\\
In Syracuse the canal museum explains\\
how a ditch in the mud built half of a state.\\
He reads every sign. He is the only one.

\songpart{Chorus}
The road runs east and he lets it,\\
one exit, one town, one coffee at a time.\\
Not every town is worth the stop.\\
Some of them are.

\songpart{Verse 3}
Utica he can't explain:\\
a road race named for boilermakers,\\
an arts institute with a better collection than it needs,\\
and a plate of chicken riggies he orders for the story.\\
Albany's capitol took thirty years to build.\\
He respects that. He climbs the stairs slowly.

\songpart{Bridge, spoken}
Case one: on the way. Rochester, Syracuse, Utica, Albany, Springfield.\\
Case two: a detour that pays. Niagara, for the water.\\
Saratoga, for the springs and the track.\\
Lake George, for a fort from 1755 and a lake too cold to argue with.\\
Case three: a detour for the sake of it. Hartford.\\
He goes anyway.

\songpart{Verse 4}
Springfield keeps the basketball hall of fame\\
and a garden of bronze for a man named Seuss, who was born there.\\
In Hartford a man who wrote about rivers\\
kept a house with more rooms than a family needs.\\
He pays for the tour. He asks one question. Nobody knows.

\songpart{Chorus}
The road runs east and he lets it,\\
one exit, one town, one coffee at a time.\\
Not every detour is worth the stop.\\
Some of them are.

\songpart{Verse 5}
Boston is last and the least surprising.\\
A red brick line on the sidewalk tells him where to go.\\
It is the only thing on the trip that does.\\
He follows it down to the harbor and the tea ships\\
and sits there until the light changes.

\songpart{Final Chorus}
The road ran east and he let it,\\
one exit, one town, one coffee at a time.\\
Not every mile was worth the stop.\\
Most of them were.

\songpart{Outro, spoken, instruments out}
He had a list from somewhere.\\
It named Springfield as a capital.\\
He checked. It isn't.\\
He kept the stop.
\end{lyrics}
