Two Bottles of Wild Turkey
Ricochets, ch13 The Glades (flashback) - Boynton Beach, 2004-04-19
Source: wildturkey.txt (Overleaf Ricochets) and the bar scene in ch13
Voice: John Carlson, second person ("you"), as in the source text
Style: slow southern-gothic outlaw country, baritone male vocal, slide guitar, brushed snare, low upright bass, swampy reverb, barroom piano, spoken asides, half-time, D minor

[Intro]
[slide guitar, ice in a glass]

[Verse 1]
She files the briefs in West Palm Beach for a Miami firm,
and every time they send her south, she's slower to return.
She comes home to Boynton shining like a borrowed light,
more radiant, more distant, every Thursday night.

[Verse 2]
So you hired Marion Gebhart, a quiet woman with a lens.
She found a junior associate, a lunch that never ends.
A hand that almost touches, a look that lingers there,
but nothing for a judge to read, and nothing you could swear.

[Pre-Chorus]
Nothing for the divorce court,
nothing for the custody fight,
just a feeling in your stomach
and a ceiling every night.

[Chorus]
Two bottles of Wild Turkey, one already gone,
two glasses on the table and the TV left on.
Your friend has had his practice making deals across a drink.
You never learned that trade, son. You just think you think.

[Verse 3]
Then an old friend called from far away, from the days at Tolemaida,
when you taught their men to chase the ghosts across the cordillera.
It wasn't social, wasn't kind: "There's a target on your wife,
and on that lawyer that she sees. Somebody wants their life."

[Verse 4]
And you said, "I couldn't care less. Let 'em do what they will.
Hell, I might just do it for 'em, if I had the will."
Then you remembered all the talk you'd spilled around the shop:
a jealous husband with a grudge: you'd be the first they'd stop.

[Chorus]
Two bottles of Wild Turkey, one already gone,
two glasses on the table and the TV left on.
Your friend has had his practice making deals across a drink.
You never learned that trade, son. You just think you think.

[Bridge]
[spoken, slurred, close to the mic]
There's a parking lot beside his office. No cameras on the corners.
There's a Colombian at the Port, Müller, Inter-Flota, everybody knows.
Put him there, put his people there, put the lawyer and her there.
If something happens, it's just business. Wrong place, wrong time.
Not targeted. That's the point.
Thursday I'll be working. No phone. People all around me. Clean.
[sung]
And he just watched you, cold and still,
said "Too many variables, like a bad film."
Then he slid a phone across the wood:
[spoken, Morocho's voice, calm]
"Whatever you decide... dump it on Wednesday."

[Verse 5]
[quiet, guitar and bass only]
You woke up with a hammer turning slow behind your eyes,
the burner on the nightstand where the empty bottle lies.
You went over every word you'd said, the parking lot, the hour,
waiting for the shame to come, the morning to turn sour.

[Final Chorus]
Two bottles of Wild Turkey, and the second's gone dry,
the plan you made while you were drunk looks sober in the light.
You turned it over one more time like a coin you couldn't spend,
and you decided it was still a good plan, my friend.

[Outro]
[spoken, almost whispered]
It was still a good plan.
[slide guitar fades; a phone buzzes once, unanswered]
[End]
