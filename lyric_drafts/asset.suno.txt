Genre and Vibe
Primary Genre: Latin Dance-Pop meets Nu-Disco.

The "Noir Groove" Element: This is achieved through minor-key chord progressions and a moody, syncopated bassline that keeps the track feeling slick and shadowy rather than purely upbeat.

The "Miami" Infusion: Built on the rhythmic foundation of 2000s-era Miami house, characterized by punchy percussion, prominent cowbell, and a heavy, driving "DUM dum" kick-drum pattern.

The "Irish" Twist: This is likely integrated through the use of a melodic counterpoint—perhaps a synth-lead or a processed violin/fiddle line that uses modal scales or rhythmic triplets, weaving in and out of the Latin percussion.

Instrumentation
The Foundation: A heavy, floor-shaking four-on-the-floor kick drum (the "DUM dum" pulse).

The Groove: A thick, slap-style electric bass or a deep synth-bass that provides the Latin jazz swing.

Percussion: A dense layer of congas, timbales, and agogô bells to keep the rhythm fast-paced and fluid.

Harmonics: Bright, stabbing brass sections (trumpets/trombones) for the Latin jazz flair, tempered by atmospheric, dark synth pads that provide the "noir" cinematic texture.

Vocal Delivery
Style: Fast-paced, fluid, and breathy. Inspired by the Gloria Estefan approach, the vocals act as a percussive instrument rather than a theatrical monologue.

Production: The voice is tightly compressed and tucked into the mix, utilizing short-tail hall reverb to keep it "in" the space of the music rather than sitting "on top" of it.

Flow: No hanging lines or dead air; the vocal phrases end exactly where the next drum hit or melodic turn begins, creating a seamless, endless loop of sound.

Technical Specs
BPM: 122–126 BPM. This range is the "sweet spot" for that Miami club energy—fast enough to get people moving, but steady enough for the Latin syncopation to breathe.

Danceability: High. The track relies on a "driving" rhythmic feel where the instrumentation dictates the pace, and the vocal delivery rides the pocket of the groove.
