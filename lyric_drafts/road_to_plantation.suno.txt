The Road to Plantation
Ricochets, ch6 Flashbacks - from Jackson Memorial, Miami, to Marta's house in Plantation, 2004-04-22
Source: ch6, Sarah's change of plans, the shooting in the Downtown garage, and Marta driving John Carlson home to Plantation
Voice: Marta, Sarah's sister, first person; Sarah only in the spoken intro (a phone call)
Style: acoustic Americana ballad, fingerpicked steel-string guitar, upright bass, brushed snare, pedal steel and a lone fiddle; spoken intro over traffic and a car phone line; warm, raw female alto with a light Miami Cuban-American color, breaking in the bridge and settling at the end; slow, 70 BPM, A minor, last chorus lifting to C major

[Intro]
[spoken, a woman's voice on a car phone line, traffic and horns behind her; South Florida Cuban Spanish]
Marta, oye, soy yo.
Chica, el turnpike está trancado, esto no se mueve pa' ningún lado.
Y mi colega todavía metido en la corte, y la reunión no puede esperar.
No me da tiempo de pasar por tu casa.
Me llevo a la niña conmigo, me tiro por la noventa y cinco derechito pa' Miami.
No te preocupes, ya está mejor, va dormidita atrás.
Te llamo cuando salga. Un besito, mija.
[call ends]

[Verse 1]
The phone rang twice, I thought it was her,
calling to say the meeting was done.
A man said "ma'am," a man said "police,"
said "Jackson Memorial," said "come."
He said "your sister," he didn't say "Lilith,"
and I held on to that the whole way down.
She was asleep in the back of the van,
she was halfway to my house, she was safe and sound.
They met me in the hallway, a doctor, a nurse,
and they said it, and I heard what they said, and I shook.
Not Sarah. Not her. She's on the turnpike,
she's stuck in the traffic, she never took
the ninety-five, she was coming to me,
the baby's asleep in her seat,
not her, not them, not the two of them,
not under the sheet.

[Chorus]
And it's a long road to Plantation,
twenty-some miles in the dark,
with the radio off and the windows up,
and the man who was her husband in the passenger's seat.
It's a long road to Plantation,
and nobody says what's true.
I'm driving the road she should have driven,
the road she didn't take, the road she didn't take.

[Verse 2]
Why'd you bring her, Sarah, why'd you bring her,
fever or not, why'd you bring her down?
You were always in a hurry, always one more thing,
always one more meeting in that town.
And you, John, where were you, where were you?
They called you, she called you, the school called you too.
Three messages, four, and you never called back,
you were raising a glass with your crew.
Up in West Palm Beach with a glass in your hand,
calling it a good day's work.
If you'd picked up the phone, you'd have known where she was,
you'd have known, you'd have known, and it still would have hurt,
but you'd have known.

[Chorus]
And it's a long road to Plantation,
twenty-some miles in the dark,
with the radio off and the windows up,
and the man who was her husband in the passenger's seat.
It's a long road to Plantation,
and nobody says what's true.
I'm driving the road she should have driven,
the road she didn't take, the road she didn't take.

[Bridge]
[tempo drops, pedal steel alone, voice breaking]
If the turnpike had moved, if the court let out,
if I'd said "bring her by, I don't care about the time,"
if I'd driven up to get her, if I'd only been home,
if I'd only been on the line...
God, I'd give you anything, give me back just the girl,
give me back just one of the two.
And I look at you, John, sitting there, sitting there,
and I hate that it's you,
you who got to walk out of a bar into the evening,
you who didn't even know,
why do you get to be breathing in my car,
why do you get to go home?

[Final Chorus]
[slowly lifting, fiddle enters, major key]
And it's a long road to Plantation,
and the porch light's on down the lane,
and I see your hands, John, I see your hands,
shaking just the same.
You lost a wife, you lost your daughter,
I lost a sister and a niece,
and there's nothing you could have done, nothing I could have done,
nothing to give us peace.

[Outro]
[car slowing, engine off; spoken-sung, quiet]
I'm sorry, John. I'm sorry for the things I said.
Come inside. We'll make up the bed.
Don't drive back tonight to an empty house,
there's a room, there's a light, there's a door.
Come inside, John.
Nobody should be alone tonight.
Nobody should be alone anymore.
[End]
