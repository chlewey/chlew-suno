Can You Come Get Me?
Ricochets, ch8 High Stakes - Chorro de Quevedo, then a tienda in Normandía, Bogotá, 2005-07 (one week; window 2005-07-07/07-28)
Source: ch8, Carlson's afternoon in the tienda near the Inter-Flota lot, his call to Lida André at Chorro de Quevedo, the drive with Óscar Julián, and the backpack and notebook kept by the shop owner
Voice: Lida André, first person; Carlson's lines on the phone and on the sidewalk as a second, male voice
Style: Bogotá rock ballad, 2000s Latin alternative rock, clean arpeggiated electric guitar with chorus, steady half-time drums, warm bass, Hammond organ pads, a lone tiple in the intro and outro, rain on asphalt as texture; builds to distorted guitars in the last chorus, then falls back to the tiple; female alto with a light Flemish accent, plain and unsentimental, rising to full voice in the bridge; male baritone thin and tired, filtered as if through a phone; 72 BPM, A minor, last chorus in C major

[Intro]
[tiple alone, then voices and a street guitar in the background, a phone ringing under it]

[Verse 1]
[Lida]
Chorro de Quevedo, a guitar going round,
somebody's cousin singing half a tone down.
My phone lights up with a name I typed in as a joke:
John Amerikaan.
I step off from the table,
away from the smoke.

[Verse 2]
[Carlson, filtered]
"Lida..."
[Lida]
And the line goes quiet too long.
[Carlson, filtered]
"I'm not feeling well.
Can you come get me?"
[Lida]
Not tired. Not the altitude.
I know that kind of wrong.
[spoken]
Where are you?

[Chorus]
[Lida]
Can you come get me?
Five words from a man who never asks,
who walks this city like a list of tasks.
Can you come get me?
I said I'd go home, I said it today,
and I'm already halfway on my way.

[Verse 3]
[Lida]
Óscar Julián doesn't want the story,
he just finds his keys.
A gray Aveo out of the parking lot,
Eldorado through the evening, then Normandía.
Every street turns into another street,
we read the numbers out loud,
we go round the block,
we go round the block again.

[Verse 4]
[Lida]
And there he is, on the curb by a pole,
head down, hands open, like he's waiting on a bus.
His clothes are clean. Somehow his clothes are clean.
A trace on his lip, I take a tissue from my pocket,
I wipe it like you'd wipe a child.
[Carlson]
"I'm sorry. I didn't mean to..."
[Lida]
It's fine. Let's get you up.

[Chorus]
[Lida]
Can you come get me?
Five words from a man who never asks,
who walks this city like a list of tasks.
Can you come get me?
I said I'd go home, I said it today,
and I'm already halfway on my way.

[Bridge]
[drums out; organ and the rain]
[Lida]
I don't know what it was. Something clear,
anise on his breath, I can smell it from here.
I don't know what he saw on the page,
I don't know what he's hunting, or what it's worth,
or who he was before he came down here.
I only know he called,
and I came,
and that's the kind of thing that makes you stay.

[Verse 5]
[guitars return, quiet]
[Carlson]
"My backpack. I left it at the tienda."
[Lida]
So I walk back in. The tables are bare.
"Mi amigo dejó un morral aquí."
The owner reaches under the counter:
[spoken, older man]
"Sí, aquí se lo guardé.
Y también dejó este cuaderno sobre la mesa."
[Lida]
A notebook, closed, and heavy in my hand.
[spoken]
¿Le debo algo?
[spoken, older man]
No, tranquila.

[Final Chorus]
[full band, distorted guitars, key change]
[Lida]
Can you come get me?
Five words from a man who never asks,
and I didn't ask him anything back.
Can you come get me?
I said I'd go home, and I still will,
but not tonight. Tonight I'm here.

[Outro]
[band drops; tiple, rain, a car door shutting, an engine starting]
[Lida]
He's in the back seat, eyes half closed,
I hand the backpack over,
the notebook inside it.
I don't open it.
I don't open it.
[tiple alone, rain fades]
[End]
