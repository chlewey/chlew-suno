**PETROVNA**

---

*[STYLE BOX]*
*Folk/pagan metal. Mid-paced, processional — weight over speed. Foundation: heavy, droning down-tuned guitars and bass, slow rhythmic pulse, no blast beats. Color layer: kantele or gusli motif in intro and bridge, threading between the heavy sections like something older than the arrangement. Drumming: deliberate, tribal-adjacent in verses, fuller and driving in choruses. Vocal: clean, restrained folk delivery in Estonian verses — precise, slightly detached; fuller, rawer, more resonant in Russian choruses, as if a different register of the same person. Bridge: vocal stripped back to near-spoken, kantele prominent, guitars recede. Final chorus: fullest delivery, guitars return at full weight. Outro: drone and kantele only, fading.*

---

**Production notes:**

- Intro and outro kantele motif should be the same phrase — the song opens and closes on the oldest layer of her identity
- The verse/chorus dynamic mirrors the language dynamic: Estonian is the composed outer surface, Russian is the interior weight — the arrangement should make that felt without underlining it
- Bridge is the emotional apex despite being the quietest moment — the intercut languages occupying the same space for the first time is the structural event the whole song has been building toward
- *"see on minu oma"* should feel like a statement of ownership, not defiance — she is not incomplete; she is precisely what she built
