Alright, Soldier
Ricochets, ch4 All Roads - Müller's house above the Bogotá Savanna, 2005-07-01
Source: ch4, Carlson's Friday break-in at Müller's empty house, Lida André's call, and the Outlook folder found at dusk
Voice: John Carlson, first person; Lida André's lines on the phone as a second, female voice
Style: spy-noir trip-hop, slow broken beat, dusty vinyl crackle, deep sub bass, muted trumpet, tremolo guitar, cold Rhodes chords, radio static and tower chatter as texture; low, hushed male baritone, close to the mic, almost whispered; female voice warm and light, slightly filtered as if through a phone, with a soft Dutch accent; 78 BPM, D minor

[Intro]
[vinyl crackle, a bus pulling away, boots on a gravel path]

[Verse 1]
[Carlson]
Portal del Norte, last stop on the red line,
an intercity bus to just before the toll.
Then up the trekking path with the city at my shoulder,
notebook full of cipher, ten o'clock on the dial.
Friday, the housekeeper's ride is on schedule,
Mrs. Alma at the door and then the door is closed.
A jammer in my pocket, a pick for the service lock,
and he never changed the code. Nobody ever does.

[Pre-Chorus]
Boots off at the threshold,
gloves on, latex white,
I walk his rooms like a memory
I'm trying to get right.
Every drawer a question,
every lamp a place to hide a mic,
Nikon on my neck, click,
click.

[Chorus]
[Lida, filtered]
"Alright, soldier."
[Carlson]
And I'm standing in the glass,
the whole savanna under me, and I don't know where I'm at.
[Lida, filtered]
"Alright, soldier."
[Carlson]
She says it like a joke she's keeping,
and I keep the lie in my mouth,
latex on the hand that's holding the phone.

[Verse 2]
[phone buzzes on a hardwood floor]
Missed call, André.
I let it ring itself out, I take my time to call her back.
[Lida, filtered]
"Allo. Are you busy?"
[Carlson]
No, not at all. I'm out in a walk.
[Lida, filtered]
"I was thinking about what you told me yesterday,
your parents in Missouri. Can we talk tonight?"
[Carlson]
Tonight won't work.
[half-spoken, aside]
If she doesn't ask me why, I don't have to make it up.
[Lida, filtered]
"Tomorrow morning, then?"
[Carlson]
Tomorrow. Breakfast, the same place.

[Lida, filtered]
"Alright, soldier."

[Lida, filtered]
"Alright, soldier."
[Carlson]

[Instrumental break]
[muted trumpet over the broken beat; clouds moving, light shifting on the savanna]
[Carlson, spoken]
The view looks different now.
Same window. Different light.

[Verse 3]
[tower chatter under the beat]
Sundown, one earplug, El Dorado on the frequency,
waiting for a Lufthansa out of Frankfurt.
Windows XP, a door left open with the key inside,
mail and files and folders, and one name I can't let slide:
Nerviger Kripo.
Letters to a man named Armando Perez,
dot gov at the end of every line.
He's been selling out his people,
and I just lied to mine.

[Bridge]
[beat drops out; static, the tower clears the flight to land]
Ninety minutes.
A backdoor in the machine,
every cushion where I found it,
every surface wiped clean.
Arm the alarm, step out,
and the dark hill takes me down.

[Outro]
[beat returns, slower, trumpet fading]
[Lida, filtered, distant]
"Alright, soldier..."
[Carlson, whispered]
I told her I was out in a walk.
I was out.
I was walking.
[vinyl crackle runs out]
