After I Cross That Gate
Ricochets, ch12 Vertrek - a taxi along Avenida El Dorado, then international departures, Bogotá, est. 2005-08-22
Source: ch12, the taxi ride with Lida André to her flight home, "Something real... at least until you cross that gate" / "It'll be real after I cross that gate", Carlson spotting the Müllers at the airport, and "Please… forget about him. Just tonight."
Voice: duet; John Carlson (male) and Lida André (female), marked [male:] and [female:]
Style: dream-pop ballad, shimmering reverb guitars, slow-washed synth pads, soft brushed drums, round sub bass, wipers and city rain as texture, a distant airport chime in the bridge; female alto with a light Flemish accent, close and unsentimental, breathy; male baritone low and tired, half-spoken in the verses; voices overlap in the last chorus without resolving; 68 BPM, E major drifting to C# minor

[Intro]
[rain on a taxi window, wipers keeping time, reverb guitar]

[Verse 1]
[male:]
Calle 26, the wipers counting down,
your bag between our knees, the meter running,
the radio low, a bolero nobody's singing,
orange lights sliding over Bogotá.
[female:]
You keep your hands where I can see them,
like a man who's learned to leave no prints.
[male:]
I'm only saying what I want to say before it's gone.

[Pre-Chorus]
[male:]
Give me something real,
at least until you cross that gate.

[Chorus]
[female:]
It'll be real after I cross that gate,
when there's an ocean and nothing to prove,
when you can't drive me, can't wait, can't save me,
and you still pick up the phone.
It's not a romance, it's not a goodbye,
don't make it smaller than it is.
It'll be real after I cross that gate,
or it was never real at all.

[Verse 2]
[female:]

[female:]
