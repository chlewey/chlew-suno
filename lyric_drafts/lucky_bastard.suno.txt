Lucky Bastard
Ricochets, ch3 Take-offs - Parque de la 93, Bogotá, near noon, 2005-06-27
Source: ch3, Carlson's morning from the boardinghouse to Jairo's tip-off and the notebook entries on the park bench
Voice: John Carlson, first person
Style: alt-country, bright open-tuned acoustic and mandolin, brushed snare, warm upright bass, major key; turns darker and slower in the bridge with pedal steel, low piano and distant thunder, then lifts again; weathered Midwestern male baritone, dry humor, half-smiling; 92 BPM, G major, bridge in E minor

[Intro]
[acoustic strum, city traffic far below a window, a match struck]

[Verse 1]
Monday morning, sun on La Candelaria,
the flask on the dresser's empty, and I let it sleep.
One day at a time, that's what I keep saying,
three smokes a day, and I'm saving one to keep.
Arepa con queso off a street-cart griddle,
orange juice in a plastic cup,
ATM on Séptima, two hundred thousand,
it's eighty-five dollars, it's enough.

[Verse 2]
Red bus up the Caracas, standing in the aisle,
past the red-light windows, I look once, then I look away.
Tinto and some buns in a café on Fifteenth,
and Jairo in his three-piece says I'm looking good today.
"Muller flies in tonight, he's bringing a friend,
his daughter comes this weekend, his folks come from Medellín."
"Cuánto es, el tinto, el pan, el... el...?"
Eight thousand on the table, "guarda el cambio," and I'm clean.

[Chorus]
Blue sky over Bogotá, green on the mountain wall,
kids on the swings at the Ninety-Three, it's school break after all.
I write down what I'm hunting, and I write down what I see,
and the man I've been chasing has got more than me.
Lucky bastard,
lucky bastard,
the sun's still shining, but it won't stay long.

[Verse 3]
Noon brings the office crowd out with their lunches,
picnics on the grass and the tables filling fast.
I flip through my notebook: New York, Miami,
"meets a friend at El Dorado," "daughter," "parents," "Medellín."
Then I write it in the margin, and I underline it twice:
"A family guy?"
And underneath it, smaller, like I'm ashamed of being wise:
"Or just a facade?"

[Bridge]
[tempo drops, pedal steel, low piano]
A year on his trail and I don't know his mother's name,
just a girl who studied in Germany, Athens with her folks one summer.
I've got a photo of a dinner, him and Sandra side by side,
laughing like there was nothing left to prove.
She'd left him and she still sat easy at his table,
easier than Sarah ever sat with me.
I never knew how to sit that easy,
I never knew how to sit that easy.

[Chorus]
[lifting again, brighter, with a hint of rain on the snare]
Blue sky over Bogotá, gray behind the mountain wall,
kids on the swings at the Ninety-Three, it's school break after all.
I write down what I'm hunting, and I write down what I see,
and the man I've been chasing has got more than me.
Lucky bastard,
lucky bastard,
the sun's still shining, but it won't stay long.

[Outro]
[acoustic alone, distant thunder]
Dark clouds forming past the mountains,
it's gonna rain this afternoon.
I could swear I didn't learn that,
it just came to me like a tune.
And I grin into my notebook,
'cause I'm thinking like a local now.
Lucky bastard,
maybe me, somehow.
[thunder, rain starting]
[End]
